# Pharmacologic Management of Essential Hypertension

Uncontrolled hypertension confers substantial cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Pharmacotherapy selection necessitates individualized assessment of comorbid conditions, including renal impairment, diabetes mellitus, and congestive heart failure.

Thiazide diuretics constitute the recommended first-line pharmacologic intervention for most patients; however, clinicians should exercise caution when initiating therapy in patients exhibiting electrolyte abnormalities. Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors are contraindicated during pregnancy because of demonstrated fetal toxicity.

Adherence optimization requires structured patient education, regimen simplification, and periodic biochemical surveillance. Nonadherence frequently precipitates therapeutic failure and subsequent hospitalization.

## Monitoring parameters

- Evaluate serum potassium and creatinine concentrations within two to four weeks after initiation.
- Assess orthostatic blood pressure measurements to identify postural hypotension.
- Document any adverse effects, including persistent cough associated with ACE inhibitor therapy.

Patients exhibiting resistant hypertension despite triple therapy should receive referral to a hypertension specialist for comprehensive evaluation of secondary etiologies.
