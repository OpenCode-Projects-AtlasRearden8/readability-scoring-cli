Uncontrolled hypertension confers substantial cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Pharmacotherapy selection necessitates individualized assessment of comorbid conditions, including renal impairment, diabetes mellitus, and congestive heart failure.

Thiazide diuretics constitute the recommended first-line pharmacologic intervention; clinicians should exercise caution when initiating therapy in patients exhibiting electrolyte abnormalities. Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors are contraindicated during pregnancy because of demonstrated fetal toxicity.

Adherence optimization requires structured patient education, regimen simplification, and periodic biochemical surveillance. Nonadherence frequently precipitates therapeutic failure and subsequent hospitalization.
